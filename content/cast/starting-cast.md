# starting cast

Nine ships for the limited version. The first three under **Core** are the playtest cast. The full nine give two groups with a shared history in the novel, plus ships that connect outward when the cast grows.

None are the most famous figures in the novel, and none are obscure. A reader who knows the book should place each one, usually from a single episode.

## Core

### EF-147 The Tigress

Tavern keeper and the planner of the Dengzhou jailbreak. Loud, funny, scheming, and able to run a room. She gives the playtest cast a social centre: someone who gathers people, makes a plan, and expects it to be followed.

### HS-212 The Twin-Tailed Scorpion

The hotter of the two hunter brothers. With his aunt, the Tigress, already in the cast, the playtest ships include a real kinship from the novel rather than three strangers. Read as a pair, the brothers' sobriquets make the relationship plain before either ship says anything about it.

### EF-287 The Flea on the Drum

Thief and tomb-raider. Small, quick, funny, and impossible to keep out of anywhere he has decided to enter. His sobriquet has the right kind of strangeness: an English speaker cannot quite picture it, and does not need to. No other ship here has his comportment.

## The Dengzhou jailbreak

HS-325 The Two-Headed Snake and HS-212 The Twin-Tailed Scorpion are hunter brothers, jailed on a framed charge. Their aunt, EF-147 The Tigress, organizes the rescue from outside. EF-134 The Sickly General, her kinsman by marriage and the Dengzhou garrison officer, joins only at knifepoint: she feigns a deathbed to bring him to the inn, and when a serving officer will not do such a thing, she draws on him in his own brother's front room. Once in, he is all in. Kinship, a rescue, and a brother she never had to draw on twice.

### HS-325 The Two-Headed Snake

The elder twin: laconic, patient, and used to tracking things that do not want to be found.

### EF-134 The Sickly General

Wan, smiling, and competent. The name promises frailty and the man does not deliver it, which is what makes it unsettling rather than descriptive.

## The Jieyang river

HS-338 The Turbulent River Dragon and HS-307 The White Streak in the Waves both pulled the same traveller out of trouble on the same stretch of river. Ferrymen and salt smugglers carry people and goods between places that are otherwise cut off, so their work is already close to a courier's.

### HS-338 The Turbulent River Dragon

Keeps his own counsel, saves people without making a debt of it, and at the end refuses the settled ending offered to him and sails away to make himself a king elsewhere. The liveship most likely, eventually, to leave the brotherhood and mean it.

### HS-307 The White Streak in the Waves

Has the best sobriquet in this cast as English: five words, all image, with a slight menace under it. An ex-river-pirate turned ferryman whose strangest episode comes after his death, which gives him an alien edge the others do not have.

## On their own

### HS-226 The Blue-Faced Beast

A disgraced descendant of generals who loses the convoy he was guarding, sells his heirloom sword, and keeps trying to earn his rank back. Pride and dignity with a visible mark, and every door shut in the same way. He links outward to several figures outside this cast if it grows.

### HS-257 The Prodigal

A servant who outclasses his master at music, wrestling, languages, and marksmanship, saves his life twice, then refuses office at the end and walks away. A ship that is better than the brother it serves and leaves anyway.
