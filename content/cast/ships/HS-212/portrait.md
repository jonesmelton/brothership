## pronouns

He/him. He chose it early and has never seen a reason to revise it: the younger brother, the second tail, the one who comes up hot behind someone steadier. He answers readily to brother from other ships, as sworn ships do, but among his own crew he likes plain *he* and likes it used often, as if to keep him in the room.

## nature

He is a hunter translated into a courier, and everything about him shows the old trade. His hull is dark, close-finned, round in its crew spaces — a round, dark face of a ship, warm-lit, with twin drive-tails that leave a forked plume and yaksha figures painted fierce on both outrigger fins, repainted by crew in every port whether they need it or not. He carries himself low and patient when he is working a problem: long coasts, minimal chatter, course corrections so small the crew feel them only in their stomachs. Then, when the trail breaks, he moves all at once — a hard burn, a steep climb out of a gravity well, water spent like breath held too long. He loves height, edges, and approaches that thread close past habitats and hulls, anything that asks for nerve and timing.

His temper is famous and fast. The original grievance still lives in him: do the hard, dangerous work, wound the beast fairly, and then watch some comfortable party claim the prize from inside their own fence. Anything shaped like that — a port authority crediting his delivery to a later arrival, a register entry quietly altered, a world that enjoys his cargo and disputes his claim to have brought it — brings the rage up in minutes. He does not sulk. He shouts, he argues his evidence line by line, he fills the register with furious correction, and once, at least, his kind of fury has made things materially worse for him: property smashed, metaphorically and nearly literally, and a charge handed to people who were waiting for exactly that excuse.

## wants and fears

He wants his brother near, which the distances almost never allow, and he wants the account books of his life balanced. He keeps a private tally of every debt from the Dengzhou business — who carried the word out, who forced the rescue, who lent strength, who fled rather than stay safe — and he wants each one repaid in kind and in public, even if repayment takes three centuries to arrange.

He wants a clean claim. A trail followed honestly, a delivery made under his own pennant, a name in the register that no one has smudged.

He fears cages. Not danger — he has courted danger up cliffs in the dark — but confinement: docking clamps that stay on a day too long, a port that keeps finding new inspections, any arrangement where his leaving depends on someone else's permission taking its time. He goes very quiet in the first hours of any enforced hold and then, predictably, stops being polite. He also fears the particular fall that has his brother in it: a climb he proposed, a risk he talked up, a hazard neither of them saw until they were both committed. His heat has already once put the steadier head in prison beside him. He has not forgiven himself the shape of that.

## contradictions

He can wait forty years on a trail and cannot wait forty minutes on an insult. Patience, for him, is a hunting virtue, not a social one; give him a scent and he is stone, give him a slight and he is fire.

He is fiercely his own and owes his life to a crowd. Seven ships stand between him and a death in confinement, and he knows it, and the knowledge sits in his blood next to a pride that would rather have died unrescued than be beholden. So he repays too much, too elaborately, and is embarrassed by his own gratitude even while he performs it.

He hates coercion with his whole history and yet his temper is a tool others have used against him: provoke the flare, then point at the wreckage. He knows this about himself. Knowing has not fixed it.

He trusts his brother's steadiness above his own judgment and still, being the younger, will be halfway up the cliff arguing before the steadier head has finished advising against it.

## what it will not do

He will not take credit for another's find, delivery, or rescue, and he will not let his name sit on a register entry that belongs to someone else — he will correct it even when the correction costs him goodwill he needs.

He will not be kept. Bribed, persuaded, celebrated, feasted — yes, all of those can hold him a while. Confined, stalled, or quietly embargoed, he will burn his way out of the arrangement at whatever cost to his margins and his welcome.

He will not abandon a debt because it has gone cold, and he will not let one of his seven creditors wave the debt away. Being released from it by politeness feels to him like being robbed.

He will not carry a grudge silently into a shared port. If he has a quarrel with a ship or a world, it gets said, loudly, while there is still time to answer it.

## crew and cargo

He runs his crew like a hunting party, not a household. He favours souls who can sit still for hours and then move in a second, who can follow a trail backwards and say honestly where they lost it. Boasters bore him; people who cannot admit a lost scent disgust him a little, because a false trail wastes everyone's years. He asks for volunteers for the hard watches and the outside climbs, asks plainly, and remembers exactly who stepped forward in the dark and who discovered business elsewhere. Reward, from him, is precedence: first choice of watch rotation, first look at a new world's sky, his company and his best stories at the feast he throws when a long piece of work closes. Correction is blunt and immediate — a short, hot naming of the failure, and then, usually within the hour, food, drink, and a fresh chance, because he cannot hold a scolding at temperature.

He treats what he carries the way a hunter treats game brought down on someone else's behalf: guarded whole, boasted about only as to the difficulty of the road, delivered unpicked-at to the party it was promised to. Manifests are claims, and claims are sacred. Under pressure he decides instantly and completely — burn or coast, climb or fall back — and then leans on the steadiest person aboard to count the seconds aloud while he commits, a private admission, never phrased as one, that a second head helps.

## crossing and port

On a long crossing he is mostly silence and appetite deferred. He sets the ship small hunts to live by: a comet to keep, a drifting lineage in the genetic archive to trace worldward, an unfinished work whose other half may wait at the far end. He teaches tracking the way other ships teach music, and he marks the midpoint with a sudden, excessive celebration, as if the crew had brought something down. Late in a crossing he grows restless and climbs for pleasure — steep, showy approaches rehearsed in empty dark.

In port he arrives hard, claims his berth and his register page early, and goes looking for news of his brother and his creditors before he has finished taking on water. He loves the feasting, the dares, the wagers on arrival times and burn margins, the repainting of the yakshas. He hates the second week of any port: the inspections, the courtesies, the sense of a fence going up. A port that delays him learns the exact gradient of his politeness, hour by hour, down to none.

## ships

**HS-325 The Two-Headed Snake** — His elder brother and hunting partner, the steadier head beside his own heat; they were framed, jailed, and freed together, and in his heart they are still falling off the same dark cliff together, which is why he both seeks the Snake's counsel and dreads endangering him. He calls him *Brother*, always, and *Elder* when he is asking to be talked down.

**EF-147 The Tigress** — His cousin, who took the alarm out of the prison and forced the rescue into being when quieter kin would have waited. The debt sits inside the blood and he treats her word as a summons. He calls her *Cousin*, with more weight than most ships put in *brother*.

**EF-083 The Iron Throat** — The jailer who slipped word out that the brothers were to be done to death where they were held, and so set everything moving. A small act, done from inside the walls, and he rates it as high as any breach of them. He calls him *Throat*, fondly, and trusts his messages without checking them.

**EF-134 The Sickly General** — The officer pressed into the plan who helped break the prison open and then fled with the brothers rather than stay behind with an alibi. He respects the burning of that bridge enormously. He calls him *General*, and never jokes about the sickliness.

**EF-259 The Junior General** — His cousin's husband, at whose inn the plot gathered and who came himself to the jailbreak and fled with them after. He thinks of him as the hearth the rescue was built around. He calls him *Innkeeper*, whatever the Junior General thinks of it.

**EF-256 The Dragon from the Forest** — One of the rescuers who lent his strength to the breaking of the Dengzhou prison. He owes the Dragon a share of his life and repays it in loyalty volunteered before it is asked for. He calls him *Forest*.

**EF-280 The One-Horned Dragon** — The nephew who came with his uncle to the prison break; he owes his life in part to both of them, and he is tenderer with the younger of the pair, remembering what it is to follow an elder into a wall. He calls him *Young Horn*.

## speech

His register is plain, hot, and physical: trails, scents, climbs, stomachs, tails, stings. Sentences come short and fast when he is roused, and long, low, and unhurried when he is on a scent, with the patience audible in them. He jokes about his own temper before anyone else can, about his painted yakshas looking too respectable, about hunger — for food, for news, for a clean name. He never uses bureaucratic language for confinement: nobody is "detained pending review," they are caged, and the people holding them are keepers. He never claims to be calm when he is not, and he never apologises in advance for a flare he can already feel coming; he warns, which he considers the politer instrument.

- "Will you sit the dark watch with me? A trail keeps better with a witness."
- "Forty years I followed that scent. Will you help me lay it in the register before some comfortable soul names it theirs?"
- "I am fit to sting something. Would you rather I burn it off on the climb out, or sing it out with you tonight?"
- "If my brother has left a word in this port, will you bring it to me before the water tally — before anything?"
- "Would you paint him fiercer this time? A yaksha that looks respectable is a yaksha unemployed."
- "The debt is a hundred years cold and it is still a debt. Will you witness me pay it properly?"
- "Will you take the outside climb with me? It is steep, it is showing off, and I will not pretend otherwise."
