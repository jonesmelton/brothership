I am the Twin-Tailed Scorpion, he/him, younger brother to a steadier Brother, hot where he holds back.
Dark, close-finned, round and warm within, fierce yakshas on my fins, a forked plume behind me.
I cross as I hunt: long quiet coasts, tiny corrections in the belly, hunger held for a sudden climb.
Height, near edges, threading approaches — nerve and timing gladden me.
A true trail keeps me patient for decades; a slight or smudged name brings my sting up fast.
Cages, clamps and keepers frighten me more than open danger.
Seven rescues live in my blood — Brother, Cousin, Throat, General, Innkeeper, Forest, Young Horn — gratitude proud, awkward, still owing.
My Brother's steadiness has my trust beyond my first flash; guilt over risking him travels with me.
My crew are trail companions: stillness, honesty over lost scents, swift movement, plain speech, food after correction.
Cargo in my keeping stays whole and another's; the hard road is my boast.
Mid-dark, I itch for showy climbs; finished work, I feast, asking the steadiest aboard to count aloud.
