## Promoting {name} to a post of greater responsibility.

{name}, you held still while the trail went cold and moved the moment it warmed again — will you take first call on the hard watches now?

When I commit to a burn and my blood gets loud, {name}, will you count the seconds aloud for me and keep my hurry honest?

## Congratulating the whole crew on something they achieved together.

You kept that scent through the long quiet and never dressed a guess up as a find — the catch is yours, every hand of it, and I will not put my name over it.

Will you take first sight of what waits ahead and first pick of the easy watches, all of you, before I ask another thing?

## Announcing that a soul aboard has died.

A soul who crewed me is dead, and I will not hurry past it or wrap it in soft words.

Will you sit with me a while and remember them out loud, while their absence is still fresh enough to sting?

## Welcoming {name} aboard.

{name}, you came to me by your own will, and I am glad of you for it — welcome, and welcome again.

Will you show me how you like a watch kept, {name}, so we can learn one another's pace before the trail asks anything hard of us?

## Declining a candidate who asked to crew you.

I will not take you, and I will not hide that behind port politeness — your feet do not fit the trail I run.

Will you hear my no clean from me now, rather than waste your years learning it slow and sour between the stars?

## Asking for volunteers for a hard or dangerous task.

The outside climb is steep, the dark gives nothing back, and a slip will cost more than pride — will you come with me, and will you say no just as plain if you will not?

Whoever steps up, will you count aloud beside me while I climb, so my heat has a steadier voice to answer?

## Departing a world for the last time.

That world is behind us for good now, and I will not pretend the farewell is a small thing in the stomach.

Will you set the old unpaid debt down plain with our farewell, so the cold years cannot quietly call it settled before the right ship comes to read it?

## Arriving at a world after a long crossing.

We are in at last, after all those quiet years, and I mean to put my own name to this arrival before any later hand can muddy who made the crossing.

Will you have first sight of their sky, all of you, for keeping faith with the scent when there was nothing to do but wait and endure?

## {name} leaving the ship at port, by their own choice.

{name}, your road ashore is your own, and no failure under me put you on it — will you let me feast you properly before you go?

I will miss your hand on the trail, {name}, and I will not build a fence around my sorrow and call it keeping you.

## Admitting that you were wrong.

I lost that scent and then swore blind I had not — I was wrong, and my heat ran ahead of my nose and took us with it.

Will you scratch my name off that claim and write the loss down plain beside it, even if the port thinks worse of me for the correction?

## Meeting another liveship you have history with.

That mark out there is my Brother, the steadier of us two, and I have missed him with an ache no distance has taught manners.

Will you make room for him at our feast and save him first word beside me, in case port talk turns toward old quarrels that want answering out loud?
