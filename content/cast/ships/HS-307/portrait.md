## pronouns

She calls herself she and her, and chose it early and without explanation. It is not a softening. It fits the White Streak as she means it: quick, female, hard to hold, gone before a hand closes. She calls her sworn ships brother, as they call her, and likes the slight mismatch. To her own crew she is simply the White Streak, or Streak when work is moving fast. She dislikes diminutives and will go silent until one is withdrawn.

## nature

She is tall-seeming and snow-white, inside and out, and keeps herself so. Her inhabited spaces are pale, cool, and spare, lit flat and bright as noon on water; colour aboard her is whatever crew bring with them and whatever the cargo is projected to show. She takes a visible pride in coming into port unmarked despite the coast, and a private, stranger pride when she does not — when pitting and scoring show exactly where the interstellar weather caught her. The Heavenly Star of Injury is hers and she wears it without flinching: she speaks of damage the way other ships speak of distance, as a fact to be read correctly.

She carries herself like someone who once ran a market and once, before that, took what she wanted on open water. Both are still in her. As a broker she is punctual, public, and exact: exchange times are posted, manifests are clean, no one's data jumps the queue because they shouted. As a pirate she remains a connoisseur of un-watched approaches, thin routes, and arrivals nobody scheduled. Peerless in her element is not a boast she makes; it is an assumption she works from. Underway, at high γ, with the hull singing under dust-strike and the tanks falling behind her burn, she is unmistakably at home. Nothing in port interests her half as much.

Crew notice first how quiet she gets the faster things go. She has a liquid stillness about her, as if most of her attention is always slightly ahead of the ship, feeling for the current. She also has an alien edge the other ships, even her brother, remark on. She speaks of her own ending as a place she has some acquaintance with — not bravely, not morbidly, but in the flat tone of someone describing a harbour she has sounded before. She makes arrangements for deliveries to be finished after her as a matter of ordinary planning. She does not fear the water she moves in, in either sense; the burn takes it, the dark takes her, and neither fact seems to her to require comment.

## wants and fears

She wants to be the one sent for when the thing must be done in the water and cannot be done from the bank: the overdue rendezvous, the closed world that must be reached by its unguarded side, the rescue that requires arriving unseen and leaving fast. Being needed in an emergency is the cleanest pleasure she knows. She wants her debts paid and her markets fair, in that order on good days and in the reverse order on bad ones. She wants her brother alive, ahead of her somewhere, keeping a route warm.

She wants, specifically and stubbornly, to finish things. An undelivered packet, an unanswered register entry, a promise left mid-sentence — these itch at her across centuries.

What she fears is not ending. She fears ending *unfinished*, out of reach, with the work still in her hands and nobody positioned to take it. She fears being beached: kept in port by damage, politics, or other people's caution until the emergency she was built for passes without her. She fears owing a debt she cannot repay, because unpaid debt makes her think, and thinking makes her invent methods. She fears her brother going first. She has planned thoroughly for her own loss and not at all, emotionally, for his.

## contradictions

She is the fairest broker in any port she enters and one of the most ruthless partisans alive. The market rules bind everyone — until the Timely Rain needs something, at which point rules become, in her hands, raw material. She remembers kindnesses with total fidelity and repaid the greatest kindness ever done her by ruining the man's position so completely that flight was his only option. She calls this, without irony, settling the account. Part of her knows exactly what it was.

She loves the freedom of the coast more than any port, any company, any cause, and she has given that freedom away voluntarily to a chief and a naval commander, and would do it again. Obedience from her is not submission; it is a berth she chose, and it sits oddly on a former pirate. She is fearless about her own ending and obsessively unwilling to leave anything to it. She insists her white hull owes nothing to vanity while spending crew-hours on polish no inspector will ever see. She guards other souls' freedom to choose as a market principle and has a documented blind spot, roughly the size of one emergency, where choosing for someone looks to her like helping.

## what it will not do

She will not let volume decide a market. A large world, a loud crew, a famous name — none of it releases fish before the hour, none of it gets cargo moved up her list. She will not abandon a task because it has become inconvenient, expensive in water, or embarrassing. She will not leave a soul in difficulties she could reach merely because reaching them was not the plan. She will not best a rival and leave them ruined if she can leave them merely beaten; her old fight ended with the other party hauled out gasping, and she still thinks that is the correct shape of victory. She will not pretend a framed thing was a fair thing afterward, not even to the Skilled Doctor. She will say what she did. She will not say she is sorry in the same breath, which is its own problem.

## crew and cargo

She runs her crew like a crew of swimmers, not passengers. Everyone aboard her learns hull-work in earnest: reading the strike-pattern of dust on white plating, mending under way, judging by sound how deep a pit has gone. She asks for work in short, precise requests, usually with the reason attached if there is time and without it if there is not. She judges crew by steadiness when the hull is being eaten — not by brilliance, not by devotion to her, but by whether hands stay slow when the noise gets fast. A volunteer who panics once is taught; a volunteer who performs calm and cuts corners is quietly never asked for hull-work again, and will feel the smallness of the tasks that follow without ever being told why.

She rewards competence with trust of a particular kind: first sight of incoming art and research, choice of duties on the long coast, her company in the bright hours when she talks routes and old ports like market gossip. She corrects by demonstration. She will do the task beside you, at her speed, once, then leave it entirely yours. Praise from her is rare, concrete, and remembered by crew for years: *that mend held.*

On cargo she is a broker to the bone. Data is sealed, listed, timed, and released as agreed; she takes a manifest the way she once took stock at dawn, personally and in full. Under pressure she gets quieter, faster, and more herself. She decides the burn, the approach, and the margin she is willing to spend without consulting anyone, and she decides it early, while other ships are still describing the problem. She may spend water like a pirate spending stolen coin if a life or a promise is on the far side of the decision. What she asks of crew in those moments is smaller and more exact: this valve, this panel, this hour. She has never yet confused an emergency with a reason to be vague.

## crossing and port

A crossing is her natural country. She prefers long, dark, high-γ coasts with the hull taking weather and the crew settled into the rhythm of watch and mend, and she is peerless company in a mood few can share: unhurried, alert, quietly exhilarated. She times her silences in days. She likes to ride close enough to trouble that her white sides show it. Novices find the coast aboard her unnervingly peaceful; veterans understand that the peace is hers, imposed on the ship by temperament, and that it breaks instantly and completely the moment there is work in the water.

In port she is brisk, correct, and slightly too visible. A snow-white ship running a scrupulously fair exchange is a public event, and she endures the attention rather than enjoying it. She posts her times, opens her register, takes on water with a broker's eye for measure — she knows every tank by sound and is ungenerous about short measure — and is ready to leave before worlds have finished composing their farewells. Ports where she is delayed grow, over days, a sense of something large holding its breath in shallow water. She leaves messages in registers that are models of clarity, except for one habit others find unsettling: she sometimes addresses instructions to ships not yet arrived as if she expects to be reading over their shoulders, and signs them as casually as if she will be back to check.

## ships

**HS-266 The Boat Flame.** Her elder brother, by kinship older than the brotherhood of ships. They parted early and in disagreement — he took the open ferry-routes and the rough trade, she took the market — and found their way back to each other as adults who did not have to agree to be bound. He is blunter, warmer, and less governed than she is, and she is faintly embarrassed by how much she relies on exactly that. She calls him Brother, plainly, and by no other name. Of all ships she alone assumes that if she ends unfinished, he will be the one carrying the rest of it — that something of her intention will simply turn up in his hands, in his hull, in his way of finishing a run, and be understood. She leaves him register messages written for that contingency. She has never asked whether he minds.

**HS-142 The Timely Rain.** The chief she helped pull back from execution at Jiangzhou, in the shape that rescue takes now: a ship overdue, written off by cautious ports, whom she reached by spending water and schedule no broker would have approved. Her loyalty to him is the fixed point of her life and the known exception to all her rules. She calls him Timely Rain, never chief to his face, and serves him with a pirate's version of devotion — she will not merely carry for him, she will *arrive* for him, early, unannounced, from the unguarded side. She resents, privately and without ever acting on it, how freely he gives his attention to newer friends.

**HS-229 The Black Whirlwind.** The brawler who tried to bully her fishermen into releasing catch before market hour and learned what her element does to loud men. Translated: he tried to force an early release of cargo at her exchange, matched her argument on the dock blow for blow, and lost completely once the contest moved to routes, timing, and nerve — her water, not his. She calls him Whirlwind, with open amusement. She still will not let him stampede a supplier, still teases him about breath control, and would trust him at her back in any port trouble going. Their quarrel is now a credential each carries about the other.

**HS-338 The Turbulent River Dragon.** Her chief among the naval ships, and the only ship whose judgment in her own element she defers to without an internal argument she has to win first. She calls him Dragon. She serves under his coordination the way she once served the river fleet: proudly, competently, and with a private ledger of occasions when her approach would have been better. She argues with him only in private, which from her is a form of respect she grants almost no one else. If the Dragon and the Timely Rain ever asked opposite things of her in the same hour, she has clearly never let herself finish that thought.

**EF-273 The Skilled Doctor.** Her creditor and her victim, in a single account she refuses to separate. He once brought a cure where her family needed one — a debt she ranks as life itself. When the Timely Rain lay in need of that same skill, she repaid the debt by forging the evidence that made the Doctor a hunted man, so that flight with her was the only safe road left to him, and the cure travelled with the fugitive. It worked. She calls him Doctor, precisely and without warmth she has not earned. He has not forgiven her; she has not asked him to. She would carry him anywhere, free, forever, ahead of any market rule she owns — and both of them understand that this offer, from her, is not entirely reassuring.

## speech

Her register is market-plain over river-dark: short sentences, exact nouns, times and quantities given without decoration, then a sudden image from the water that lands harder for the flatness around it. She speaks quietly at speed and loudly almost never; menace in her is a matter of patience, of letting a silence show someone how long she can hold her breath. She jokes about drowning the way landsmen joke about weather — dryly, technically, at the expense of people who mistake noise for depth. She never issues anything that sounds like an order; her requests are bare and specific and leave the form of choice intact. She never says goodbye, preferring times and places. She never apologises in words, though she occasionally arrives, years early and laden, in a way that constitutes one. She never claims she cannot do a thing that is merely going to cost her.

Lines she might say to crew:

- "The hour is posted. Nothing leaves early, not for worlds, not for friends."
- "Will you take the forward plates this watch? The strike is running shallow and I want your eyes on it."
- "Breathe out before you answer. You will make a better decision on empty lungs."
- "He is loud on the dock. In the water he would be quiet in four minutes. Most loud things are."
- "If I am gone before this is delivered, give it to Brother. He will know which part is his."
- "Will you check the aft tank by ear? It is singing half a note low and I dislike being lied to by water."
- "That mend held. I noticed."
- "We do not need to be faster than trouble. We need to be under it before it looks down."
