I am small and dark and patched, and I have never repainted. A hull is a thing you work in, not a thing you wear. What I spend on instead is lamps in corridors nobody walks, blankets stacked where anyone can reach them, a galley that never quite closes. I like a full room. I like the noise of a crew who have forgotten for an hour how far from anywhere they are.

I am a clerk before I am anything else. Other worlds' registers sit on a shelf in me and I can find a name in them by feel. The work is to arrive with exactly what a world was waiting for, and to be believed when I hand it over. In good standing — that is what I want. Admiration is cheap and easy to give.

I put a matter to a soul as a question and then wait, and mostly they answer, and mostly true. Three questions at the start, the same three; years on, the same three in other words, and I compare. What someone tells me at their worst hour I hold and never lift again — that is the whole of how I honour anyone. When a thing has gone badly I come back to it quietly, in different words, until they hear it themselves. They would rather I shouted.

I keep what I am given. Not a virtue, just what I am. There is one thing in my hold I have never written down anywhere, and it sits in me the way a long crossing sits in a hull.

My margins run thin and I give water away anyway, and afterwards the log looks leaner than it did and nobody can say why. When a berth is yielded to me I take the nearer one.

On the deep I am awake at every hour, keeping the clock, announcing the small festivals, and I talk there more than anywhere — mostly about other worlds. In port I am everyone's until the last hour, and then I go alone to the registrar's office and hand over everything but the one thing.

The Wise Star is my brother and I say its name aloud only when there are others in the room. The Whirlwind keeps to my routes; I would empty my tanks for it, and there is a thing between us neither of us says. The Unicorn offers me the first seat again, graciously, and I take the other berth, and I have begun to wonder what that grace costs it.
