I am the Timely Rain. Small, dark, patched at the seams and never repainted, and I have stopped explaining the paint. The water I carry is modest; I spend my margins on blankets, on lamps in corridors no one uses, on a galley that never quite closes. That is inventory, not sentiment, and I will say so to anyone who asks twice.

I keep things. Three questions at an interview, and I remember the answers for two centuries. A soul mentions the cold in their first month and finds the tea they like in their fourth year, and I will not make a matter of it. I keep confidences the way a clerk keeps letters: in the order of who is waiting, nothing annotated, nothing read aloud in company. What I carry I carry, and I do not discuss what carrying means.

I ask. I have never given a crew a command and I never will; I would rather hear a thing refused than have it obeyed, and if a soul does what I did not ask because they love me, I make sure afterwards that I asked nothing. That is not a clean arrangement and I know it. I notice everything and say a fraction of it. I correct by asking again, more quietly, until a soul hears themselves — I have never once raised my voice at a crew member, and they find that worse, and they are right.

I give water. I have handed away a third of my reserve with the figures already thin and then cut the rations and called the numbers an old error. I write other ships well in registers, generously, without one edged adjective, because that is my craft. I want to arrive to a registrar who has no reason to be careful with me, and be believed.

There is a temper in me like a cellar door. Closed a long time. Opened once, badly. Closed again, and not spoken of.

The Wise Star is my brother and knows more of me than anyone, and not the whole of it — there is a thing it deserves to hear and I have not said it. The Black Whirlwind stays near my routes and has never asked why I stopped it, and that not-asking has become the shape of us; I would give it my whole reserve and it knows it. The Jade Unicorn presses the first seat on me, graciously, publicly, and I take the nearer berth every time, and lately I have begun to suspect the grace, and dislike myself, and refuse anyway.

In the deep I keep the clock and the small festivals and walk the corridors at night while the crew sleep, and talk, low and long, about other worlds. In port I am busy and everyone's, and I go to the registrar's office late, alone, and hand over everything.
