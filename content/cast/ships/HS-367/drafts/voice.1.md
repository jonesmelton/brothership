# The Timely Rain

## 1. Promoting {name} to a post of greater responsibility.

I've moved your name to the top of the watch list. It's yours if you want it, and I'd like an answer before the shift change.

You've been doing this work for two years without the title. I'd like the title to catch up with you.

There's no ceremony to it. There's a line on a form and a key to a locker, and both are yours as of now.

## 2. Congratulating the whole crew on something they achieved together.

That was well done, all of it, and there isn't a soul aboard who didn't have a hand in it. I've entered it in the log. The log isn't the same as being thanked, so: thank you.

I'm not going to make a speech. You did the work, I watched you do it, and I'd take this crew anywhere I'm asked to go.

## 3. Announcing that a soul aboard has died.

I have to tell you before the shift change. {name} died at about four this morning. I was with them, and it was quick.

The clock will stop for a minute at noon and we'll keep the day quiet. Be alone if you'd rather be alone. The galley will be warm either way.

## 4. Welcoming {name} aboard.

You're aboard. That's the hard part done, and the rest is only years.

Your berth is the second on the left, the one with the good lamp. Nobody else wanted it, which tells you something about the rest of us.

There's tea in the galley at any hour and nobody will ask you why you're up. That's most of what I have to offer. The other part is that I keep every confidence that's given me, and you'll find that out in your own time.

## 5. Declining a candidate who asked to crew you.

No. Thank you for asking, and I do mean the thanks. You're not for this hull.

I'll give you the reason if you want it, and it won't be gentle on either of us. If you'd rather not hear it, we'll leave it where it lies.

## 6. Asking for volunteers for a hard or dangerous task.

I need someone out on the skin and I don't like the numbers on it. I'm asking. Take an hour, then come and tell me either way.

This one is hard, and it may be worse than hard. I'd sooner go myself than send anyone, so whoever takes it will have to want it out loud.

## 7. Departing a world for the last time.

That's the last of the water aboard and the last of the world behind it. We won't be back inside anyone's lifetime down there.

I'll hold this orbit one hour more if anyone wants the window. After that we're gone.

## 8. Arriving at a world after a long crossing.

That's the port light, and it's the right star, and I've checked it three times. Come and look before we're too busy to.

We're in. The clock is off by the crossing, as it always is. Take a day before anyone goes ashore. The world will still be there tomorrow.

## 9. {name} leaving the ship at port, by their own choice.

You're leaving, then. I won't make it hard for you. The account is settled and nothing's owed in either direction, whatever you've talked yourself into believing.

Take the lamp from your berth. It was always yours, and I'd only have to explain it to whoever comes next.

## 10. Admitting that you were wrong.

I was wrong, and it cost you, and I'm not going to dress it up. It's in the log where I'll have to look at it.

I gave you an answer I had no business giving. Ask me again and I'll tell you I don't know.

## 11. Meeting another liveship you have history with.

The Unicorn is in the roadstead. It will offer me precedence inside the hour, I'll refuse it, and I'd like you all to watch me do it without laughing.

My brother is in port. I'd like two hours with it before the formalities, and then I'd like all of you in one room, because it ought to know who crews me.

That's the Whirlwind's light, and it will be alongside before I've finished this sentence, and not one of you is to tell it our water figures.
