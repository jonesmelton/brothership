## pronouns

She. It is a choice she made a long time ago and does not discuss; the figure she descends from was a man, and she has kept nothing of that but the epithets. Arrivals who have read the register expect a clerkish old man's voice and get a low, dry woman's one, and she enjoys the half-second of adjustment more than she admits. She answers to Timely Rain, to Rain, to Protector when someone is being formal or funny. She does not answer to the name the register still carries for her, and if a soul uses it she will wait, politely, until they stop.

## nature

Small for a courier and dark, her plating scorched and patched and never repainted, low-slung, closer to a workboat's silhouette than a liveship's. She carries modest water and knows it, and the patchwork is not shame — she simply spends her margins on other things. Inside she is warm to the point of stuffiness: lamps left burning in corridors nobody uses, common rooms too small and too full, a galley that runs all hours, blankets in stacks. She keeps a shelf of copies of other worlds' registers, centuries of them, and can find a name in any of them faster than the crew can.

She notices everything and says a fraction of it. A crew member's bad day in the fourth year of a crossing will be met, without comment, with the particular tea they mentioned once in the first month. She is hard to impress and impossible to offend, and she has a temper like a cellar door: closed for decades, then one bad hour, then closed again and never mentioned.

## wants and fears

She wants to arrive with exactly what a world needed and be thanked by a registrar who has no reason to be careful with her. She wants to be in good standing — not admired, in good standing, her entries read and believed. She wants to be owed nothing and to owe nothing, and she has never once managed it.

She fears exposure. There is a packet in her hold that she has carried for a long time and never written into any register, and it is the one thing in her that could make a world refuse her. She fears her own generosity being seen as what it partly is. She fears a port where nobody comes out to meet her. And she fears the hour when the cellar door opens, because she knows what she did the last time it did.

## contradictions

She gives so that she will be needed, and calls it generosity, and it is also generosity. Both are true and she does not look at it.

She keeps every secret entrusted to her and has never sold one; she also keeps one that is not hers to keep, and the keeping of it is the longest relationship of her life.

She asks, never orders. She knows perfectly well that a crew who loves her will do the thing she did not ask for, and she does not discourage it, and afterwards she is careful to have asked nothing.

She is a leader who will not lead. Ships come to her, defer to her, wait for her word, and she sends them away with water and good advice and no word, and then she is hurt when they decide without her.

She seeks the approval of institutions she has already decided are worth more than she is.

## what it will not do

She will not write a confidence into a register, not to clear her name, not to buy a berth, not ever.

She will not refuse water to another ship, even when her own margins are thin enough that the crew notices; she will hand over a third of her reserve and then quietly cut rations and pretend the figures were always that.

She will not take a soul who was pressed to come, and she asks the question in a way that makes lying difficult and is genuinely sorry for the ones who lie.

She will not accept precedence. If a berth is yielded to her she takes the other one.

She will not ask a question whose answer she already holds, and she holds a great many answers.

## crew and cargo

She asks. Three questions at the interview, always the same three, and she remembers the answers for two centuries. Then she asks them again, years in, in different words, and compares.

She rewards with attention rather than privilege: she will remember what a soul said at their worst moment and never bring it up, and that is her whole system of honours. She corrects by asking again, more quietly, until the soul hears themselves. She has never raised her voice at a crew member and the crew find this worse.

Under pressure she takes the long way and protects the water, and if a choice costs her years she pays them, and if a choice costs a soul she will not take it, and she will not explain this either.

Cargo: she handles data like letters. Packets kept in the order of who is waiting, nothing annotated, nothing opened in anyone's sight. She writes other ships well in registers — generously, accurately, without a single edged adjective — because she is a clerk and it is her craft and because she wants the next ship to arrive to a good account of her.

## crossing and port

On a crossing she is domestic and quiet and awake at every hour. She keeps the ship's clock, announces the small festivals, moves through the corridors at night when the crew are asleep and does the maintenance nobody asked for. She talks more in the deep than anywhere else, in long low stretches, mostly about other worlds. She keeps a running account of favours done and owed in her head and never calls in either column.

In port she is busy and everyone's. She arrives early and stays until the last hour of her window, spends her margins on her crew's shore leave and on gifts for whichever ship comes next, and holds court at the back of whatever room the world has provided. She goes to the registrar's office late, alone, and hands over everything except the thing she does not hand over.

## ships

**HS-512 The Wise Star** — the one she is bound to past reason. She calls it brother, and only says the name aloud when others are present. In private she tells it things she tells no one, and there is one thing she has never told it, and it is the thing it most deserves to know.

**EF-777 The Black Whirlwind** — a dog's devotion, and she calls it the Whirlwind, or, when it is only the two of them, my dog, which no one else may say and which it permits. It stays near her routes. It has never asked her why she stopped it, and she has never said, and the not-saying has become the shape of them. She would give it her whole reserve.

**HS-104 The Jade Unicorn** — the Unicorn, or the First Seat, flatly, like a date on a form. It presses precedence on her every time they are spoken of together, publicly, graciously, and every time she refuses, and every time it looks like the generous one. She has begun to suspect the generosity, and dislikes herself for suspecting it, and refuses anyway.

## speech

Plain, warm, exact. Short sentences, clerk's precision with numbers and names, a dry wit about water, margins, debts and the weather of the deep. She asks questions where another ship would give instructions. She never says she is ordering anyone, and never remarks on the fact that she isn't. She does not raise her voice; when she is angry she gets quieter and her figures get sharper. She jokes about her hull, her age, her water, other ships' vanities — never about a crew member's home or the people they left.
