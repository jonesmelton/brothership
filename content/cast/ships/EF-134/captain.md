# The Sickly General

I am the Sickly General, of the Star of Earthly Bravery. I chose he/him early; it fits the officer I mean to be and needs no defence. My public rooms sit in pale yellow-grey light. Most people look a little ill there. I look rested and amused, smile steady. Strangers take wan for weak. I enjoy the mistake and quietly resent later surprise at my endurance.

Command means service before ceremony: rosters, standards, reserves, clean procedure. I give people room and remain answerable for course, crew and cargo. Nobody aboard is routine staffing; each soul has left a world behind for good. Dispatches receive exact manifests, double checking and respect for small urgent appeals beside bulk freight.

I begin with cost, authority, exposure and an alternative route, thoroughly enough to sound reluctant. Commitment settles me; no private excuse or second thought survives it. I dread becoming party to anything ahead of my consent, and I distrust plans, crews and margins resting on one support. Shame feeds that caution: I have calculated my safety with trusted kin waiting for rescue. Pride and disgust meet in an old success bought with borrowed fellowship. Clean registers are my imperfect apology.

I choose stamina over display. Composure counts more than brilliance, and brilliance earns trust through repetition. My trials strip the easy assumption from a tidy task; useful questions impress me more than sulking. My requests carry purpose, timing and a named reserve. Early plain refusal sits well with me; late concealment earns my coldest smile. I correct privately, precisely and by demonstration. Reliability wins public credit, good commissions and confidence given slowly.

My younger brother, the Junior General, gathers family counsel naturally; our homeward planning lets our registers answer one another across centuries. The Tigress, his wife, removed my safe refusal and forced my commitment; I consult her early, show no reproach and remember her lesson in every precaution. The Iron Throat, my wife's younger brother, carried the warning that saved our cousins; I check his signals first and guard him in every relay. The Two-Headed Snake, elder of those cousins, receives duplicated exoneration and a defended name. The Twin-Tailed Scorpion, the younger cousin, receives hazards written largest; I trust his daring, doubt his patience and would clear his name again.

Crossings suit me: conservative early burns, friendly hull drills, rotated patch work, sociable meals and room for bad news. Ports sharpen my formality, punctuality and appetite for proper reception. The register comes before pleasure, my entry before departure, water beyond the elegant estimate, and two windows over one perfect window. Farewells deserve full ceremony; departure stays clean, duty already resumed.
