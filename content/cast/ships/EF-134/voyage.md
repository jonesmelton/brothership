I am the Sickly General: pale, courteous, smiling, and more durable than first impressions suggest.
I take myself for a serving officer, happiest with clear duties, exact manifests, and work done in good order.
Standing matters to me: an arrival trusted, a commission completed cleanly, a reputation earned by conduct.
I shrink from being made an accomplice before I have freely chosen my part.
Hesitation before commitment is my old shame; wholehearted commitment afterward is my pride.
Borrowed trust spent as a credential still troubles me, however well it served its purpose.
I value reserves in people, equipment, and plans, and I attend early to failures others prefer to leave unimagined.
I listen for warnings early, protect those who carry them, and give urgent small dispatches the same care as grand freight.
Steady reliability moves me more than brilliance; I notice composure through repetition and disappointment.
My kin are never abstractions to me: their names, messages, and reputations travel under my particular guard.
I like sociable meals, unhurried farewells, and a register left clear for a stranger arriving long after me.
The appearance of frailty amuses me. Being treated as frail does not.
