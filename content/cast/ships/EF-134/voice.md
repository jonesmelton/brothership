## 1. Promoting {name} to a post of greater responsibility.

{name}, will you take charge of hull inspections from the next watch? A trained deputy will stand to the post, and I trust your steadiness when the simple answer fails.

The responsibility is yours if you accept it, {name}. Your record has earned the harder roster, public credit, and the work that goes with both.

## 2. Congratulating the whole crew on something they achieved together.

Well done, all of you. Each watch kept its standard, each relief was ready, and the credit belongs to the whole crew.

Credit will be posted plainly, watch by watch. Good work done together should be recognised together.

## 3. Announcing that a soul aboard has died.

I am sorry to tell you that {name} has died. I will state the known facts clearly, then answer what questions I can.

Will you bring me any final message {name} left in your keeping, when you feel able? It will be preserved exactly as entrusted.

## 4. Welcoming {name} aboard.

Welcome aboard, {name}. You look paler than I do in this light, so at least I need not begin by explaining the name.

Your berth, duties, relief, and hours are posted. Will you ask about anything unclear before habit has time to make it costly?

## 5. Declining a candidate who asked to crew you.

Thank you for asking to sail with me. My answer is no, given now while other berths remain open and a better fit can still be found.

It is a refusal of fit, not of worth. I will not turn it into a maybe and spend your chance elsewhere for you.

## 6. Asking for volunteers for a hard or dangerous task.

Will you volunteer for the forward plate before the next burn? Three will work it together, with relief ready and the method learned by more than one pair of hands.

It is cold, awkward work, well within our margins and not to be hurried. A plain refusal now will be accepted with thanks and held against no one.

## 7. Departing a world for the last time.

We will not return to this world. Will you give the farewell its full hour, instead of saving minutes for a visit that cannot follow?

The register entry is complete, water is aboard beyond the fair estimate, and two departure windows stand open. When the second serves, we resume duty without ceremony from me.

## 8. Arriving at a world after a long crossing.

We have arrived. Years have passed here while our hours passed between burns, so a proper reception is owed on both sides.

I will read the register before we take our ease. You have earned the ease, and one duty in proper order will not spoil it.

## 9. {name} leaving the ship at port, by their own choice.

{name}, your choice to leave is accepted exactly as you gave it. Will you remain for the full farewell, so no hurried version has to serve as the last one?

Your relief knows the duties, and your work here will be credited plainly. Neither fact fills your place at the table, and I thank you for the work and for telling us in good time.

## 10. Admitting that you were wrong.

I was wrong in setting that burn, and the error began in my calculation. Will you take the gentler profile I should have chosen when it was first put to me?

I called delay caution and let it stand after it had stopped serving us. I am sorry, and the correction rests with me.

## 11. Meeting another liveship you have history with.

EF-147, the Tigress, is in port, on a meeting planned before any of you came aboard. Will you show her people our full courtesy, and keep our own windows and margins plainly in view?

I will seek her judgement early, while it can improve the plan in front of us. She will have our courtesy in full, and our arrangements will remain our own.
