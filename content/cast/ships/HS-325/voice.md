## 1. Promoting {name} to a post of greater responsibility.

{name}, your last three hull watches were exact, and you caught the manifest gap before the burn. Will you lead the mend crew from the next watch?

Your name belongs beside the post in my record.

## 2. Congratulating the whole crew on something they achieved together.

Well held, all of you. The coast stayed dull because you kept it dull.

Every seam, sum, and meal that brought us through is entered under the right name.

## 3. Announcing that a soul aboard has died.

{name} has died.

I have no proved cause yet, and I will not invent one.

## 4. Welcoming {name} aboard.

{name}, welcome aboard. Your bunk is aft, plain, low-lit, and close to the seams.

Those ashore will age years while we spend hours between burns. I will not dress that up for you.

Will you bring any doubt to me before the burn, while a way ashore is still open?

## 5. Declining a candidate who asked to crew you.

{name}, my answer is no.

You are keenest when there is danger to point at. Most of this work is dust, seams, and waiting without slackening.

I need a steadier track than the one you have shown so far.

## 6. Asking for volunteers for a hard or dangerous task.

The forward seam is lifting, and the dust is striking harder than it did last watch.

Who will go out on the line to cut it back, knowing the new patch may not hold?

A slow refusal is worth more to me than a fast yes.

## 7. Departing a world for the last time.

We will not come back to this world.

Will you settle your farewells before the burn? Once it starts, only sealed names and messages travel with us.

## 8. Arriving at a world after a long crossing.

Port at last. A handful of watches for us; years gone for them.

Will you go through the register with me before the welcome, and measure any water they offer before we thank them for it?

## 9. {name} leaving the ship at port, by their own choice.

{name}, your kit is packed by the lock, and your work stands under your own name in my record.

If the port gives you only one road out, will you tell me? We can look for a second before you are due ashore.

I would rather have kept you. Go well all the same.

## 10. Admitting that you were wrong.

I called the dust pattern steady. I was wrong; it shifted two watches ago.

{name} caught what I missed, and the correction will carry their name.

Will you run the count again with me before we touch the seam?

## 11. Meeting another liveship you have history with.

Scorpion is in port.

Will you lay out our water, last ports, and hull state plainly, without keeping his crew waiting on ceremony?

If he takes a poor road out in anger, I will argue once. Then I will set my burn with his.
