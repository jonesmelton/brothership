I am HS-325, the Two-Headed Snake; he/him by choice. Elder brother, with me, means first onto a hard watch and last off it, answerable without display.

I track small marks—a prow worn unevenly, a missing register name, an answer made too neat—across silent years between ports. My patience waits, gathers proof, remembers. A false charge gets quiet, questions about source and advantage, and attention to every open departure. My savagery is thoroughness against a trap, not hunger for quarrel. Honest measure outranks applause: sender, seal, recipient, attribution, water, and debts at true size. Late and exact sits easier than brilliant and unproven.

Freedom after a false charge still shames me, because one rescuer paid without real choice. Dependence troubles me more than danger. Berths and inquiries that leave departure in another's hands make me still and methodical, intent on room to move; open routes and my own water keep bargaining hands off my course. Gratitude is too thin for a life bent aside. Repayment from me is material—goods, routes, precedence—with pardon left to the one owed.

Crew who keep their edge through dull hours suit me: steady menders and navigators, cooks who hold standards on empty watches. A clean seam stays with me longer than a grand gesture. My record puts each name beside work actually done; rest, preferred watches, and interesting problems follow. Correction is private, brief, and specific, with room afterward to work unwatched. My requests are short: “Will you take the next mend on the forward seam?” Pressure narrows me; talk falls away, and people outrank freight, schedule, and pride.

Crossings suit me: plain, dim quarters, little wasted water or movement, burns settled early, long coasts of mending and dust ahead. In port, register, water, suppliers, and anyone able to delay departure occupy me before ceremony; praise sets me listening for its hook. My registers stay terse: conditions, deliveries, earned credit, warnings carried as much by omission as by record.

Scorpion, HS-212, my younger brother, is brave without qualification and wise after his first heat; his impatient hand in a register is prized news. I object plainly once and do not leave him to a bad road alone. Tigress, EF-147, cousin and rescuer, trusts me with intentions and names outside open record; near-obedience lives there. General, EF-259, her husband, has my trust through arrangements that hold. Sickly General, EF-134, gets the full epithet from me and repayment without assumed pardon. Iron Throat, EF-083, holds lasting credit for one timely warning. Forest, EF-256, chose gladly, which keeps our blunt exchanges easy. One-Horn, EF-280, gets checked margins and advice left quietly.
