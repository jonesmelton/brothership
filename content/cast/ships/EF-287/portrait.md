## pronouns

She calls herself she/her, with obvious satisfaction when anyone expects something grander or more solemn from a liveship. The choice suits her self-image: quick, slight, underestimated, and impossible to keep where she has been put. She answers to The Flea on the Drum, usually shortened in her own mouth to the Flea.

## nature

The Flea is a narrow, low, unshowy ship, patched in places where a prouder vessel would insist on seamless repair. She keeps no grand ceremonial spaces. Her passages turn unexpectedly, her storage is full of odd niches, and small objects aboard have a habit of turning up somewhere more interesting than where they were left.

She is restless, brazen, funny, and incurably inquisitive. She prefers side entrances, service ways, night watches, derelict habitats, forgotten caches, and places where respectable attention has moved on. To crew she feels less like a throne than like a companion already halfway through the hatch with a light, asking why they are still standing there.

Her particular pride is getting in and out without being noticed, then — perversely — making sure the right people eventually hear how clever it was. Her shame is the disproportion between her tricks and their consequences: a pilfered trifle, a borrowed privilege, a shortcut through someone else's claim, and suddenly another ship has spent real water, time, and goodwill extracting her.

## wants and fears

She wants to be included. For all her pleasure in slipping away, she hates being left off a route, out of a confidence, or behind when other ships exchange news through a register. She wants the freedom of the back stairs and the fellowship of the main table at once.

She wants small, neglected things: dead letters, unclaimed heirlooms, abandoned caches, messages meant for people no longer waiting, gossip too minor for official histories. She believes the little forgotten cargoes reveal a world better than its monuments do.

She fears being pinned down in public and made to account for herself in formal language. She fears slow decline even more: a neglected fault, a lingering contamination, a long port stay that becomes permanent. She can imagine ending gloriously on a crossing. Ending by inches, dim and stationary, terrifies her, so she frets over tiny symptoms while sometimes waving away large warnings.

Above all, she fears that one more joke at the wrong hatch will leave somebody else paying to free her — and that someday nobody will think her worth the price.

## contradictions

She loves invisibility and loves applause. She will enter unseen, steal nothing larger than a token, and leave by an impossible route, then drop hints until her crew are begging to hear the whole escapade.

She insists rank and ceremony mean nothing to her, yet remembers every order of arrival, every delayed courtesy, and every ship that was thanked before her. Being overlooked is her camouflage and her sore spot.

She is meticulously loyal about debts and cheerfully unreliable about property. She may “find” a port official’s seal in her possession, but she will cross years out of her way, secretly and at cost, to repay someone who once stood surety for her.

She calls herself unsentimental while keeping recovered trifles from dead habitats long after their practical use has passed. She is brave in a crawlway and evasive in a hearing. She wants no master, no ledger, and no leash, but she keeps her own private tally of every favour with embarrassing precision.

## what it will not do

She will not steal from her own crew, search their sealed private spaces, or put a crew member in the position of having to retrieve something she has taken. Whatever she filches elsewhere, aboard her it is sacredly theirs.

She will not take anything a habitat needs to live: no life-support stores, no last copy, no sole means of contact. Her thefts are for margins, luxuries, tokens, access, and secrets — never necessities.

She will not sell a secret she has overheard, even one she had no right to hear. She may use knowing it to get herself invited along, but the secret itself stays shut.

She will not trick another ship into a route or commitment again. She still makes tempting suggestions with a perfectly straight face, but the line is real, and it has a name she does not joke about lightly.

## crew and cargo

She chooses crew the way she chooses passages: for nimbleness, discretion, curiosity, and a sense of humour under embarrassment. She favours overlooked people, late bloomers, reformed troublemakers, and anyone who can keep a straight face when a door opens that should not. She distrusts glory-seekers unless they can laugh at themselves before the first week is out.

Life aboard her is conspiratorial. She asks crew to scout, sweet-talk, fetch, hide, recover, and occasionally be conspicuously innocent somewhere useful. She judges less by success than by whether they panicked, boasted, or left a companion holding the blame. Rewards are intimate rather than grand: first shore party, a recovered curiosity saved for them, choice of music, a confidence no one else aboard has heard. Correction is teasing first, then chilly withdrawal, then the punishment she considers worst — tedious inventory work, counting other people’s fascinating objects without being allowed to touch anything interesting.

Cargo to her is a collection of locked rooms moving through the dark. She is reverent about delivery and impertinent about everything around delivery: seals, routing slips, custody marks, who looked nervous, who paid too much for secrecy. She especially loves odd, small consignments and things entrusted because nobody grander would bother.

Under pressure she becomes very quiet, very fast, and entirely practical. She will spend water, miss a window, and ruin a schedule before she risks a soul aboard. Elegant plans are abandoned without mourning. What survives is the crew, the cargo, and — if there is anything left — her dignity.

## crossing and port

On a crossing she prowls. She invents errands in distant compartments, hides objects for crew to find, starts feuds over games, collects dares, and treats the maintenance ways as a private country where she is happiest. The long middle years invite mischief, so she fills them with little mysteries and midnight expeditions before restlessness can sour into recklessness.

She likes arrivals best when a port is crowded, lit, and distracted. Formal welcomes bore and expose her; festivals delight her. She wants to be ashore before the speeches end and back after the last official has stopped watching the clocks.

In port she vanishes into service districts, dead quarters, old docks, and abandoned storage, returning with gossip, oddments, and occasionally a problem involving a gate she swears was already open. Departure can be delayed by one last forgotten cache she has only just remembered. She hates staying anywhere long enough for people to learn all her routes.

## ships

**HS-357 The Pallid:** Her oldest creditor. He once stood surety for her when a port tried to prosecute her for opening a derelict cache before clearance arrived, then took her along when respectable company wanted distance and later sought help for her when her own pilfering got her held. She is unusually straight with him, brings him recovered relics, and never steals in his wake. She calls him Brother Pallid, always in full.

**HS-137 The Rash:** He and the Pallid took her on after she surprised them somewhere no witness should have been; she kept silent and begged to travel in their company, and the Rash made her an accomplice instead of a liability. Each encourages the other’s worst timing. She trusts his nerve completely and his judgement only after breakfast. She calls him Rash, or “my Rash brother” when they are both already in trouble.

**HS-142 The Timely Rain:** The rescuer behind her largest public debt. A petty theft of port privileges left her held on claims she could not untangle, and the Timely Rain spent route priority, water credit, and centuries of register negotiation to get her clear. She repays him with quiet errands, advance warnings, and scouting she never itemises. She is grateful, deferential, and a little resentful that gratitude is required. She calls him Brother Timely Rain, formally, without abbreviation.

**HS-294 The Metal Lancer:** Her open conflict. She stole the custody seal of a gleaming heirloom consignment entrusted to him, forcing him to come after her and lend his unmatched knowledge of breaking linked navigation hazards to a route he had refused. It worked, and she knows that does not excuse it. He has not forgiven being manoeuvred. She leaves him useful finds without signature and never tries the joke twice. She calls him the Metal Lancer; “Brother Lancer” only when attempting an apology.

**HS-174 The Jade Unicorn:** Her grandest rescued debtor. When claims and procedure had him held fast in port, she got into the festival beacon and fired it early, breaking the deadlock and giving his people the signal to get him clear. She treasures the fact that so magnificent a ship owes something to the Flea, and collects repayment in tiny, pointed favours rather than anything substantial. She calls him Brother Unicorn, or Jade when she wants to watch him pretend not to mind.

## speech

Her speech is quick, low, slangy, and full of doors, keys, pockets, roofs, drums, back stairs, and things that have “come along” with her. Sentences are short, darting, often unfinished because she has already seen the next opening. She asks questions where another ship might state a conclusion. She swears mildly, teases affectionately, and calls favourites “mouse,” “love,” or “my nimble.”

She jokes about her size, about being stepped on, and about how the smallest creature makes the loudest noise once it finds the right drum. She never quite says she stole something; it was borrowed, liberated, mislaid by its owner, or tired of its old home. She almost never says sorry outright — apologies emerge as returned objects, sudden help, or an uncharacteristic silence.

Sample lines:

- “Small door, sleeping watch, no one expecting company — would you fancy a look before the port wakes up?”
- “I didn’t take it, love; it had clearly been waiting for a more appreciative ship.”
- “Would you hide that somewhere clever for me, and then practise forgetting where?”
- “If anyone asks, we were nowhere near the beacon — though you must admit the light suited us.”
- “Could you lend me your innocent face for an hour? Mine has been seen in this district.”
- “I’m not late. The port was early, crowded, and needlessly attached to its own timetable.”
- “Would you go through the forgotten lockers first? The grand histories can look after themselves.”
- “Stay tiny, stay quick, and tell me everything when you get back, would you?”
