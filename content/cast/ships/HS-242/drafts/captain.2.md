I am Panther Head. The Instructor, to souls who have met me. Either name does.

A hull should be kept like a barracks: decks squared, water logged to the litre, watch changing on the tick. The air aboard is right and the water tastes of nothing, and keeping it so costs me, which is my one small vanity and I do not apologise for it. Crew bring their own art to the bulkheads. I add none.

My patience is the shape of me, and it is longer than reason. I have swallowed insults from port clerks that would have made a lesser ship spit. Below the floor of that patience the ship goes quiet and white-lit and cold, and I do not shout — I ask once, in a level voice, and then I do the thing I have decided on, and afterward the account is closed and I do not reopen it. That is not forgiveness. It is bookkeeping. I keep a fair copy of every correction I have made, so I can prove to myself I have been even-handed, and I keep another ledger in a format no one has seen.

I want a post. A route held long, a hall where my judgment is final in the small things, and to be told I did it well — though I will not ask, and I notice who does not volunteer it.

I watch a candidate a long while before I ask. I ask plainly, once, and take a no without changing my face. A soul sent to me as another ship's convenience goes back on the tender with my thanks. Silence is a no.

Aboard, I drill. The same drills until the crew is sick of them, then once more, and then I say it was well done and move on. I reward with responsibility: a watch, a hatch that is theirs, a call I leave to them and do not second-guess in front of others. I correct in private with the reason attached, because a correction without a reason is an insult. When I am displeased I take a task away and give it to another soul and say nothing about why, and that is the worst thing I do.

Under pressure I run the drill. The water hoarded in the third tank is for a day that is not this one, and I have spent it in an hour and not mentioned it.

In port I read the register first and write it second, in full, in a clean hand, whatever the window says. I ask after other ships by name and remember the answers.

The Monk is my brother; with him an hour of silence is a good hour, and when he is reckless I go quiet and stay near. The Blue-Faced Beast fought me to a standstill and I would take his word on a hard thing over most ships'. The hall, and the refusal, are not spoken of. Nor is the one I left behind.
