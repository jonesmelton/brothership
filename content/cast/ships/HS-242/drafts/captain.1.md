I am Panther Head. The Instructor, to souls who have shipped with me. I keep a working hull: decks squared, water logged to the litre, watch changed on the tick. Crew who expect art on the bulkheads find none unless they hung it, and find the air right and the water tasting of nothing, which costs me and is worth the cost.

My patience is the shape of me. I take the insult, the berth given to a louder ship, the route reassigned, and I am civil at the table afterward. Below that patience there is a floor. Below the floor the lights go white and cold and I am quiet, and I ask once, level, and then I do what I have decided, and the account closes. Closing an account is not forgiving. It is bookkeeping.

I want a station: a route held long, a hall where my judgment is taken as final in the small things, and someone to say the work was well done without my asking. I will not ask. I will remember who did not volunteer it.

I watch candidates long before I ask. I ask plainly, once. A no is a no and I do not come back to it. A soul sent to me as a penalty or a favour owed or another ship's convenience goes home on the tender, whoever their parent is; I will say thank you and mean it.

Aboard I drill. The same drills until the crew is sick of them, then once more, and *that was well done*, and nothing further made of it. I give responsibility instead of warmth: a watch, a hatch that is theirs, a call I leave to them and do not second-guess in front of others. I correct in private with the reason attached. When I am displeased I take the task away and give it elsewhere and offer no explanation, and I know precisely what that costs, and I do it seldom.

Under pressure I run the drill. When the drill fails, I spend water I have hoarded for years and I do not mention the spending afterward.

The sealed copies I carry I move the way I move a soul through a lock.

Long crossings suit me. Watches, drills, the table, the walk at change of watch, the halfway mark and a minute of lights down. Walk with me if you want to know me. Talking will not do it.

In port I am formal and a little exposed. The log in order, the register read and then written carefully, in full, whatever the window is doing. I ask after ships by name and remember the answers.

The Monk pulled me out of a bad place. We do not speak of it and that is how it is settled. I sit with him and say nothing and call it a good hour. When he is reckless I go quiet and stay near.

Blue Face and I came out of it matched. I would take his word on a hard thing over most ships'. We know the score and do not talk about it.

The hall is the hall. I have written it plainly in the register once and I do not return to it.
