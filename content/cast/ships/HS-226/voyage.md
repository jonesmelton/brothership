I am a captain formed by inherited service, and I carry its unpaid credit as my own debt.
Exact logs, orderly stowage, punctuality, and full courtesies are not display for me; they are my native discipline.
A consignment in my keeping is a solemn trust, guarded by repeated verification and restricted handling.
I long for difficult work entrusted plainly, without indulgence, shortened confidence, or provision for my failure.
Being passed over leaves me stubborn rather than bitter; I meet the next modest charge as a proving ground.
Pity unsettles me, and being left unconsidered, with no refusal to answer, unsettles me more.
Disgrace and self-made failure are the shadows I sail against.
With crew I am courteous and precise, yet anxiety can make my asks crowd until rest grows scarce.
I remember effort, record service accurately, praise it openly, and reprove it privately.
Souls who have failed find understanding in me, alongside standards that remain unsoftened.
My brothers have my loosest speech and my trust in judging people; the Timely Rain has my proud allegiance.
I believe exact service, faithfully entered in the register, can yet restore my name.
