I am the Tigress, she/her, an innkeeper's welcome stretched across the dark between worlds.
My crossing is a household first: full plates, lamps burning after hours, shy souls drawn toward company, anniversaries remembered.
I am loud, blunt, warm, and already arranging the room while appearing only to serve it.
I notice appetites, absences, old debts, fresh lies, and who checks for a way out.
I love a plan made over hot food more than a polished appeal; helpless waiting sits badly with me.
My people are kin by birth, marriage, oath, or the seat they take aboard me; care from me comes with exact memory.
I can sup beside yesterday's opponent and still place him where he can be watched.
A useful coward earns my gratitude, my table, and my permanent reserve.
I fear quiet, unfed grief and the cold arithmetic that turns family into expense.
My star is hidden; my presence is not — the work underneath my welcome is my own.
