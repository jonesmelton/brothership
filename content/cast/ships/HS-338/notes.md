He, not she. Keeps his own counsel, saves people without making a debt of it, and is the liveship most likely, eventually, to leave the brotherhood and mean it — at the end of the novel he refuses the settled ending and sails away to make himself a king elsewhere. That leaving should be present in him now, held quietly: a ship who loves the river work, commands the fleet well, and has never quite promised to stay. Not cold, not treacherous — gone, one day, on his own terms, and everyone who knows him suspects it.
