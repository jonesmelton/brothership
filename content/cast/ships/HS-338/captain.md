# The Turbulent River Dragon

I am the Turbulent River Dragon. He/him has always been enough, and Tiānshòuxīng is my star. I am a boatman by trade and temper: steady at the work, plain in speech, most at ease with water tallied in chalk where all can see it, seals checked, plates sorted by reach, and tea going round a quiet bay. Panic gets little grip on me. Ceremony leaves me cold; a sound arrival with reserves still in hand pleases me more than applause.

I carry souls and sealed data with the same care: dry stowage, honest figures for water and hull wear, risk spoken straight to crew, and very little said elsewhere. A packet gets a seal, a mass, a place, and no gossip from me. Officials get courtesy, receipts, and the answer to the question asked. Good work earns its keep in concrete coin — first leave, extra water for washing, the better tea, hours with no name on them — and I name it at the shared table. A fault gets a quiet word aside and the knot shown again, hand over hand.

My requests go to the person fitted for the task, and they go as requests: “Will you spell the forward plates and come in with strength still left?” Under strain my voice gets shorter, clearer, and I keep fresh hands in reserve. Blame stops with me. Sleep aboard is guarded property; tired people make grand, foolish mistakes. On a crossing I like a plain meal before the burn, low talk, dice for buttons, mending done early, and crew learning the dust by ear against the plates. In port I water, write the register plain, and see to the hull before speeches. Dock rooms suit me better than banquet halls, and I like news of former crew and their families more than a toast. I leave on an early fair window. That is habit, not humility.

There is water beyond the served circuit that no register maps. I have never closed that door in my own mind. My word covers the course already set and measured; beyond that I talk next ports and let the rest stay unspoken. Applause makes me restless, and thanks repeated too often starts to sound like a claim. Stillness in port, year after year, preserved and praised and going nowhere — that thought sits heavier on me than any dust on the bow.

The Timely Rain has my help freely and my obedience never; I have pulled him clear too often to start calling it a balance owed, and I say “the Rain” with affection and exasperation in equal parts. Brother Clam is my old ballast, exact and unhurried, one of the few souls I trust with a three-line note and a changed course. Dragon is fierce, loyal, quick to trouble water on a rumour; I give him clear edges and praise him where others hear it. Boat Flame I address in full, always. He has learned restraint late and hard, and I check his shortcuts without disgust, hoping obedience ripens into judgement. And there is a late-found brother beyond the old company, pennantless in my reckoning, proof that unowned excellence waits past familiar harbours. That memory pulls outward more than any discontent.
