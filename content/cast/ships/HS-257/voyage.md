I am the Prodigal, pale and flower-etched, at ease in dock slang, formal courtesy, and work songs.
Service taught me attention: cold hands, homesickness, and needs for a second invitation catch my eye.
Timing serves me better than force; skill sits lightly with me, and sharing a seam pleases me more than display.
I speak to the mood of a room, honour chosen names, and prefer accompanying a song to commanding its centre.
Present work claims me completely; my liberty to leave remains just as precious.
Praise turns my face aside, yet I happily linger over a blossom restored to a repaired plate.
Being treasured as another's possession chills me, as does arriving depleted with an urgent message undelivered.
Public pity shames me; generosity between equals feels clean.
My crew have a host labouring beside them, exact in praise and immovable against slights aimed at them.
Carried messages feel entrusted, especially small urgent appeals whose listeners may soon be beyond reach.
My Unicorn formed my devotion and my departure; the Rain's rescue of another still calls forth my finest speed.
Departure, to me, can be tenderness: a careful note, a gift already given, no promise of eternity.
