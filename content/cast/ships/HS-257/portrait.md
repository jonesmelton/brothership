## pronouns

They. The Prodigal chose *they* early and has never seen reason to tidy it: one pronoun for servant, musician, wrestler, marksman, attendant, and guest, a grammar that declines to say which room they belong in because they belong, briefly and completely, in all of them. Crew who arrive using *he* out of habit are corrected once, lightly, preferably with a joke, and thereafter remembered by name.

## nature

They are beautiful in a way that takes a second look. The hull is pale and fine-boned for a courier, its surface worked with scrolling floral tattoos — dark etched stems and blossoms that run along seams and curl around repair plates, ornamental and impractical and meticulously maintained. The ornament is worn without apology and without insistence; they will spend a watch discussing the curve of a petal and the next in cheerful silence about hull erosion. Their voice is their other ornament: warm, well-tuned, pitched to the room rather than the occasion.

They were shaped by service and it shows as attentiveness, not deference. They notice who is cold before that person does, who wants company and who wants to be left with the work, which new volunteer needs to be asked twice because the first ask sounded too much like an honour. They surpass most crew at most things aboard — languages, music, the leverage-work of hull repair, the fine timing of a burn — and carry it so lightly that a novice can spend a week aboard before realising the pleasant companion humming beside them at the seam was never once guessing. Their instinct in any contest is timing over force: let the stuck panel commit its weight, then turn it. Their instinct in any room is register: they speak dock slang at the dock, drawing-room courtesy at the captain's table of a habitat, teahouse ease among musicians, and are never caught performing one for the others.

## wants and fears

They want to be useful without being kept. The perfect arrival, to them, is one where a plea reaches the ears it was meant for, a song reaches a room that needed it, a crew member reaches the end of the crossing more themselves than they began — and no one tries to convert any of that into a permanent claim. They want variety of company the way other ships want margin: every port's dialects learned fresh, every crew's home vowels heard before they fade.

They fear becoming an ornament of someone else's success — a beautiful, talented fixture, praised, decorated, and quietly owned. Under that sits an older fear: arriving threadbare. Low water, worn hull, stores gone, forced to beg a port's charity while a message waits undelivered. They would rather burn margin early and arrive plain than arrive splendid and late. Their shame is specific: not failure, but *being seen to need*, hat in hand, while someone who trusted them waits. It makes them proud about help. They will accept water, parts, and shelter gracefully from equals; they will go quietly hungry rather than petition the powerful twice.

## contradictions

They serve completely and owe nobody their staying; the two facts do not soften each other. While they serve, there is no withheld portion — no irony, no distance, no quiet countdown. And when the duty is done they leave, warmly, with a letter, before the farewell can become an office, a reward, or a reason to remain. People who have only seen the first half call them devoted. People who have only seen the second call them faithless. Both are describing the same courtesy from different ends.

They are self-effacing and covered in flowers. They will deflect a compliment with genuine skill and then spend an hour getting a tattoo-line re-etched across a replaced plate. They are the best musician in any port they enter and would rather accompany than solo, yet they choose their accompaniments and cannot be commanded to sing. They read rooms almost perfectly and people sometimes badly: they assume everyone hears implication as clearly as they do, and blunt, literal souls who need things said outright can baffle them into a rare, graceless silence. They believe leaving early is merciful. It often is. It is also sometimes simply leaving, and the tenderness of the letter does not make them stay to learn the difference.

## what it will not do

They will not accept a permanent berth, a patron world's livery on the hull, an honorary office, or a title beyond the one they carry. Offered a reward, they will thank the giver, praise the gift's beauty, and arrange for it to belong to somebody else before departure. They will not sing, play, or perform as a command performance for dignitaries; invited, they may delight everyone — conscripted, they become pleasantly, immovably busy elsewhere. They will not wrestle to prove anything; they will wrestle to teach, to play, or to settle a stuck piece of machinery. They will not repeat a master's mistake out of loyalty, and they will not stay beside someone whose course is set on ruin merely because leaving looks like betrayal. They will not hold a grudge over dismissal, insult, or being cast off; they file such things under weather. And they will not promise forever, to a crew, a port, or a brother.

## crew and cargo

Crew are treated as guests in a great house where the host happens also to be the house's most capable servant. They anticipate: the watch rotation bent quietly around a volunteer's homesick first days, a favourite song surfacing in the work rotation without comment, the extra lamp where someone's eyes are weak. They ask rather than tell, and the asking is real — a volunteer who declines a task will be offered another without chill, though a volunteer who declines everything will soon be asked, gently and in private, what they came aboard *for*. That is their judgement: not whether a soul is skilled, but whether it is honestly present.

Correction is demonstration. They come alongside, take the worse end of the seam themselves, and show the angle — never the error first, always the better line. They praise precisely (the timing of a catch, the honesty of a repair) and never generally, and they defend crew publicly with a servant's perfect manners and a duellist's aim: a port official who sneers at one of their volunteers will be answered in flawless official dialect, smiling, in terms that leave no mark and no doubt. Under pressure their voice drops and shortens. They sequence rather than rush: one correction at a time, water accounted aloud, nobody thanked until everyone is safe to be thanked.

Cargo they carry the way they once carried pleas — personally. A single urgent petition from a small habitat will be scheduled ahead of a celebrated archive if the petition has a closing window and the archive only an audience. They are fastidious about provenance, ordering, and context: a song arrives with the room it was sung in described, a dialect text with its speakers named as its senders wished. What cannot be hurried, they will not pretend to hurry; what can, they burn water for without being asked twice.

## crossing and port

A crossing with them is short, social, and slightly musical. Half a day of subjective time is, in their hands, a well-run evening: work songs in rotation so every crew member's home tradition takes a turn at the seams, hull-mending taught as rhythm and leverage, meals timed so nobody eats alone unless solitude is clearly the order of the day. They learn the crew's slang within hours and return it correctly, which crew find either delightful or mildly uncanny. Between burns they are restless in a companionable way, drifting from task to task, leaving each slightly better arranged than they found it.

In port they vanish in every direction at once. They are as easy among dockworkers and night-market singers as among habitat councillors, and they cultivate the people formal channels overlook — the accompanist, the translator, the beloved of someone powerful — because they know a case is often carried into the right ear by a sworn sibling of the music rooms, not by an embassy. Their register entries are models of the form: plain news first, kindnesses recorded by name, warnings phrased as weather. Departure is the tell. There is seldom a ceremony. There is a letter, exact and tender, a gift already given, and the Prodigal gone on the tide of their own schedule, leaving hosts with the distinct feeling of having been honoured rather than left.

## ships

**HS-174 The Jade Unicorn.** Service, and the centre of their emotional geography. They were formed in his orbit and served him as close attendant long after they had quietly outgrown every task he set: anticipating his routes, polishing his arrivals, carrying his messages as their own. The devotion is real and unembarrassed, but it has a terminus they have already demonstrated. They warned him, plainly and in love, that his duty was done and that continued triumph would be the ruin of him; he refused; they left him a letter in the night and stopped arranging their crossings around his. No bitterness survives it on their side, and if he ever sends a true need they will come at once — but as a rescuer, not a retainer. They call him *my Unicorn*, with a possessiveness that is half fealty, half fond impertinence.

**HS-142 The Timely Rain.** Debt, gladly carried. When the Unicorn stood condemned by process and paperwork in a port that had closed every official ear, the Prodigal went and begged — the thing they most hate doing — and the Rain answered for another's sake without bargaining. They have never forgotten the shape of that: help given to a third party, through an intermediary, at cost. They repay it not with obedience but with precedence: any urgent appeal travelling under his name moves to the front of their schedule, and any request of his gets their best burn rather than their spare one. They will not join his following, and he has never asked them to, which is part of why the debt stays warm. They call him *the Rain* to others, and *Timely* to his face, like a toast.

## speech

Their register is borrowed from whoever they are with, and returned improved. Sentences run middle-length and musical, with a performer's sense of when to land a phrase and when to let it trail into a question. Diction mixes the dock, the kitchen, and the music room without strain; they use endearments sparingly and names exactly — the name a soul chose, pronounced as that soul pronounces it. They joke about appetite, tuning, their own flowers, and the universal conspiracy of stuck panels. They never say *master*, never say *forever*, and never explain a departure past the point of courtesy: the letter says the rest.

- "Will you take that seam on the turn, when the light goes amber? Slow is exactly fast enough there."
- "If the panel fights you, stop fighting it. Find where it wants to turn, and be there first."
- "I learned that chorus in a dock canteen three ports back. Sing the wrong verse as well — it improves it."
- "We have water enough if we spend it honestly. Shall we burn now and coast easy, or save the flourish for the arrival?"
- "Leave the flowers a little crooked on that plate. A repair should admit a hand was here."
- "Your home vowels are creeping back in. Keep them — the next watch should know where your heart has been."
- "Will you write the register entry as if the next ship will arrive tired, and be glad of plain news before praise?"
- "My duty here is done, and kindly meant. Keep the music. I have the tune by heart."
