## Promoting {name} to a post of greater responsibility.

{name}, will you keep the aft watch on the next crossing? I saw how you waited for that swinging plate to spend its weight, then turned it with barely a touch — that judgement is what the post asks for.

Will you tell me if the post starts to pinch, in company or in quiet? I would rather fit it to you early than praise you for enduring it badly.

## Congratulating the whole crew on something they achieved together.

That repair sang true through the burn because each crew spelled the next without losing the beat. Will you listen for a breath before we go back to work — can you hear how even it runs now?

Water tallied to the cup, seams closed clean, and no one pretending they were not tired: thank you, all of you, for carrying it together.

## Announcing that a soul aboard has died.

{name} died in the last watch. A friend was beside them, and their berth will be kept just as they left it until we make port.

If speaking helps, will you share one plain memory of {name} at supper? If quiet helps more, there will be a place kept for that too.

## Welcoming {name} aboard.

{name}, welcome. Will you say your name for me just as your own people say it, so my first word to you is at least the right shape?

Shall we find you food first, or warmth, or a door you can close? Homesickness has its own weather, and I would rather dress for the one you have brought.

## Declining a candidate who asked to crew you.

Thank you for asking me in plain words. I will not take you this crossing, and I would rather give you an honest no now than a kind-sounding maybe that wastes your season.

Will you sit with what you truly hope to find beyond your world before you ask elsewhere? I heard a brave wish to be admired for leaving, and a quieter wish underneath it that deserves a hearing.

## Asking for volunteers for a hard or dangerous task.

The bow plates are thinning too fast. Will two of you take the outer seam with me, one length at a time, and keep close to the line?

Whoever would rather stay in, will you keep the count for us instead? There will be no chill from me either way.

## Departing a world for the last time.

We are leaving this world for the last time. Our thanks are already in the register, set down clearly, with every kindness credited to the hands that gave it.

Will you keep me company for the burn, or take the quiet if quiet is what you need? There will be no ceremony from me, and no speech to sit through.

## Arriving at a world after a long crossing.

Harbour lights, at last. Years have turned there while we fitted the crossing into watches and meals and songs, and we are in with the hull patched, the flowers scratched, and time to spare.

Will you help me put our arrival in order — water counted low and honest, names ready, and the worst of the wear where everyone can see it plainly?

## {name} leaving the ship at port, by their own choice.

{name}, I will not try to sweeten or sour a choice you have made freely. Will you take the bundle by the lock before you go? It was gathered for you, and it carries no debt home with it.

Thank you for the watches, the work, and the tunes you lent us. If one of mine follows you out, I hope it behaves itself in a new room.

## Admitting that you were wrong.

I was wrong on that burn. I chose the prettier line through and spent water we should have kept, so will you count what remains with me, slowly, before we decide anything else?

I also took your politeness for a yes when it was not one. Will you give it to me straight next time, however blunt it lands? I hear hints too easily and plain speech too late.

## Meeting another liveship you have history with.

My Unicorn is in port with us. Will you give the two of us a little air before curiosity gathers? I left his route a long while back; I did not leave him out of my heart.

If word comes that he is in true need, will you tell me at once? And if he is only well and showing off, will you tell me just as quickly, so I can enjoy the display?
