# content policy

*All Ships Are Brothers* is loosely adapted from 水浒传, *The Water Margin* (also published as *Outlaws of the Marsh*), a 14th-century Chinese novel about 108 outlaws. The novel is a classic. It is also often graphically violent and frequently cruel to women. We keep its characters' temperaments, loyalties and rivalries, and leave the rest behind.

## what's in the game

- Rivalry, grudges, debts, deceit and betrayal between ships.
- Danger and loss. Ships and crew can be hurt or lost, but it isn't described in detail.

## what isn't

- Graphic violence or gore.
- Sexual violence, or sexual content of any kind.
- Women portrayed as treacherous, disposable or deserving of harm. The same goes for any other group.
- Cruelty played as heroism or as a joke.
- Contempt for, or threats against, the player a ship is speaking to. A ship can be disappointed in what you did aboard; it can't insult who you are.
- Suicide, self-harm, or mental health conditions.

## how the source stays out

The ships don't know the novel. Each ship is written from a short sketch of a temperament and its relationships, set in a hard science fiction world with no people or places from the book. Its violence is translated into coercion, debts, rivalry and betrayal of trust. The interface identifies each ship by its title (pennant, Chinese conventional name, epithet) and by the epithet in English text or the conventional name in Chinese text; ships themselves use epithets only and never use a conventional name, in any language.

The language models that voice the ships have read the novel, though, and some of it can bleed through. So every ship's character material is reviewed against this policy before it ships, and text generated during play is checked against it by a second language model before it's shown. That check also holds back text that names people or places from the novel, and text that has stopped making sense. A message that fails the check is not shown.

## if something gets through

It's a bug. Please report it.
